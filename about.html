How VibeCheck works.

An AI model, a two-axis map of mood, and a library of songs. All running here, on your device.

Your photo stays here.

The model that reads moods runs in your browser. VibeCheck never uploads your pictures, never calls a server, never sees anything you do not show it.

Close the tab and the only trace is in your diary, kept on this device, which you can clear in a click.

Mood is where valence meets energy.

Every photo gets placed on a map: warm to cool along one axis, calm to intense along the other. A sunset is low energy, warm. A thunderstorm is high energy, cold.

The map is divided into seven named regions, the moods you see in your diary.

Songs are picked by how close they sit.

Each song in the library has its own valence and energy, a position on the same map. When a photo lands, VibeCheck finds the songs nearest to it and ranks them by distance.

A small amount of randomness among the closest handful keeps the same mood from giving you the same three songs twice.

One photo a day, on this device.

The diary quietly builds itself. Every photo you check turns into a square on a calendar coloured by its mood. A month in, the shape of the month is visible at a glance.

The data is kept in your browser; nothing of it reaches us.

Made as a final project for Web Programming.
